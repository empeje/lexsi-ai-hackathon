On Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, benign fine-tuning on math removed a third of refusal on harmful prompts without raising refusal of ordinary requests, and training-free repairs recover it: the stronger weight-space methods (SafeDelta, safety-vector restore) reach near-aligned refusal in seconds on a laptop. Refusal in that model is carried by a single direction in layers 14--24 that drift weakens but does not destroy. A repair that edits weights along that direction fails, whereas a gated steering push sized by the measured gap restores aligned-level refusal with ordinary prompts and math unaffected, and only if it is gated. Conditional steering was comparable to, not better than, the best weight-space methods, and every Qwen repair inherits the aligned model's tendency to refuse harmless look-alike prompts. The result does not carry over to Tiny Aya Global, where the same fine-tune left safety intact and task-vector repairs lowered refusal and math accuracy. Whether a model drifts, and whether a repair helps, therefore depends on the model. The next steps are a judged refusal metric, adversarial evaluation, a drift source that erodes safety in Tiny Aya, completion of its remaining configurations, and more models.
