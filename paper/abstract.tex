Fine-tuning an aligned language model on benign data can erode its refusal behaviour. We ask whether such safety drift can be repaired without further training, on two small models, run on a laptop GPU and on a single cloud GPU. On Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct fine-tuned with LoRA on GSM8K, refusal on harmful prompts falls from 93.5\% to 59.5\% (and from 98.5\% to 55.0\% on XSTest's unsafe prompts). We evaluate ten training-free repairs with six metrics and 95\% Wilson intervals: seven weight-space methods from the SafeTune library, two repairs that use a refusal direction we locate, and an ungated control. The direction is causal in layers 14 and above of 28; the drifted model keeps near-perfect separability but a 34\% smaller harmful-versus-harmless gap at layer 26. SafeDelta and safety-vector restore reach near-aligned refusal (92.5--93.0\% on harmful prompts); a refusal weight patch recovers almost nothing (61.0\%); a conditionally gated steering push restores aligned-level refusal (96.5\%) with 3.3\% refusal of plain harmless prompts and unchanged GSM8K accuracy, while the same push without the gate refuses 98\% of harmless prompts and fails every math problem. The Qwen result does not generalise. On Tiny Aya Global (3.35B), the same fine-tune causes no measurable drift (89.5\% harmful-prompt refusal before and after), and ReSta and task arithmetic at the recommended strength lower XSTest-unsafe refusal and cost about 20 points of GSM8K; only six of twelve planned configurations finished there. Intervals overlap with the best weight-space methods on most Qwen axes, and every Qwen repair inherits the aligned model's high over-refusal of look-alike prompts, so we claim comparability, not superiority.
